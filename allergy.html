Allergic airway disease

Allergy in the airways runs from the nose to the lungs, and it is usually one continuous problem rather than several separate ones. Persistent sneezing and a blocked nose, a cough that returns every year at the same season, and asthma that flares in particular months are commonly parts of the same picture.

What it usually feels like

  • Bouts of sneezing, an itchy or blocked nose, a clear runny nose
  • Itchy or watery eyes
  • A tickle in the throat and a dry cough, often worse at night
  • Loss of smell, or a heavy feeling across the face
  • Symptoms that follow a season, a room, a journey home, or contact with an animal

Common triggers in and around Hyderabad

  • House dust mite — the most frequent indoor trigger, concentrated in mattresses, pillows and soft furnishing
  • Pollens, which follow a seasonal pattern
  • Fungal spores, particularly in damp rooms and after the monsoon
  • Pets, and cockroach allergen
  • Non-allergic aggravators such as traffic pollution, incense and cigarette smoke, which worsen an already sensitised airway

Why the nose is checked in a lung clinic

Untreated nasal allergy is one of the commonest reasons asthma stays poorly controlled despite reasonable inhaler treatment. Treating the upper airway often improves the lower one. If you have both, they are managed together rather than by separate visits to separate people.

Assessment

The pattern of symptoms usually points to the trigger before any test does — which months, which rooms, what changes when you travel. Where identifying a specific allergen would change management, testing can be arranged: skin prick testing, or a blood test for specific IgE. Spirometry is done where there is cough, wheeze or breathlessness, to check whether the lower airway is involved.

Management

  • Practical reduction of exposure at home — targeted at what you actually react to, not a list of everything
  • Nasal steroid sprays, used correctly and for long enough to work; technique is checked in the room
  • Antihistamines and other medicines as appropriate
  • Inhaled treatment where the lungs are involved
  • Referral for immunotherapy in selected cases

A note on long-term relief medicines: nasal sprays that clear a blocked nose within minutes are decongestants, and using them for more than a few days causes rebound blockage that is worse than the original problem. Please bring whatever you are currently using so it can be reviewed.
